# Contribution assessment

## Central scientific account

At gain r=0.5 ln(1+8 alpha lambda), the complete residual quantum state has a uniform norm-controlled limit. It gives a phase-averaged TMSV in the physical frame: exact number statistics become asymptotically indistinguishable from the deterministic-pump prediction, while the joint state remains finitely mixed. The mean pair number is 2lambda sqrt(N) to leading order, proved with a separate energy estimate. A fixed pump-purity threshold occurs at gt=[ln(alpha)+ln(8lambda_epsilon)+o(1)]/(2alpha), rather than the fixed-degree polynomial-root extrapolation in the closest same-model analysis.

The consolidation closes the measurement-interpretation question without adding a mode or error channel to the generation dynamics. The rescaled squeezed EPR quadrature has the explicit non-Gaussian characteristic function exp(-xi^2/4)/sqrt(1+2lambda^2xi^2). A bounded two-frequency identity gives a nonzero asymptotic trace-distance lower bound from *every single Gaussian state*, including mixed and retuned states. This is a corollary of the state limit, not a separate physical mechanism or a new class of non-Gaussianity witnesses.

## Strongest positive interpretation

The result determines which of two apparently compatible modeling successes are actually compatible: complete number-statistical accuracy and a Gaussian quantum-state approximation. They separate in one controlled dynamical regime, rather than through arbitrary state examples. The complete state limit and explicit uniform remainder support bounded measurement predictions, and the separate energy proof avoids using trace distance to infer unbounded moments. The onset comparison makes a precise statement about a published extrapolation while preserving its short-time coefficients and finite-range numerical usefulness.

The new readout is more discriminating than a purity number alone. It proves that retuning a deterministic Gaussian state or merely fitting a mixed Gaussian covariance does not remove the state-level discrepancy. It also clarifies which measurements fail to detect it: total-number-conserving measurements remain accurate, whereas a suitably phase-referenced quadrature distribution does not.

## Strongest skeptical interpretation and boundaries

The physical mechanism is established quantum-pump phase noise and number-sector information. Gaussian interaction frames, displaced-number structure, non-Gaussian quadrature noise, and logarithmic high-gain limitations all have antecedents. This pass expanded the [KFD] reading from a publisher abstract to readable author-draft prose; it reinforces that the quantum-pump squeezing limit and the danger of finite asymptotic extrapolations cannot be claimed as new. The complete old Hillery–Zubairy article remains unread. None of these access boundaries proves originality.

The measured variable is standardized on a shrinking physical scale, exp(-r)=Theta(N^(-1/4)). A fixed nonzero additive readout resolution eventually erases this particular homodyne distinction. Ideal phase references and adequate phase stability are declared resources of the measurement, not supplied for free by a traced-out pump. No specific device operating window, metrological advantage, or failure of a particular photon-counting application is established.

The output can be approximated by an explicit convex mixture of Gaussian states, even though not by a single Gaussian state. No Wigner negativity, Gaussian-hull separation, or ready-made non-Gaussian computational resource follows. The cubic joint pump–output state and the reduced Gaussian mixture must remain distinct.

The mathematical error estimates are conservative. They establish the asymptotic regime, not that moderate experimental brightness automatically gives small certified error. The fixed nondegenerate model is not a solved multimode/lossy device.
